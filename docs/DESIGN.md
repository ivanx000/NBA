# Design spec: Tip-Off

## 1. Visual direction

**Mood:** a dark, empty arena right before tip-off. Cinematic lighting, team colors as the only saturated color on screen. Think broadcast intro, not cartoon.

- **Players are holograms with real colors** (decided Nov 2026, replacing the line-art holograms; the look follows the hologram site in the user's promo video and the `kyledev223/lions-share` repo). Each player is lit like a real person (skin, shoes, his team's actual jersey colors from `matchupJerseys`) and then turned into projected light: fine, dark, see-through scan lines drifting slowly up the body, a glowing rim in the team's hologram color with a crisp bright edge at the silhouette, a brighter band sweeping up now and then, a rare flicker, and every ten seconds or so a quick glitch where a few thin slices flash (done in the fragment shader; moving vertices would fight the depth pre-pass and speckle the surface). The lit surface is held under the bloom threshold so faces and lettering stay crisp; only the rim and arena lights glow. Only the surface nearest the camera is drawn (a depth pre-pass), so the back number never shows through the chest. Tuning lives in `HOLO` (`components/scene/tint-material.ts`).
- **Room: an arena on game night, in real-life colors** (decided Nov 2026; replaced the arena drawn only in points and the platforms, then a version with screens and colored searchlights). **The court follows a real NBA home court** (`components/scene/court.tsx`), drawn unlit so it shows in bold, solid color with no glare or fog (a glossy, lit floor mirrored the room's light as a white sheen and looked faded); lines and lettering are a soft white just under the bloom threshold, so they stay crisp. Maple boards with grain and joints; the lanes, the apron all round and the center circle painted in the home team's primary color; white lines with every marking (lane blocks and hash marks, the restricted-area arc, free-throw circles solid outside the lane and dashed inside, the three-point line, coaching boxes at 28 ft and the substitution box on the far side); the home city and nickname lettered along the baselines and the nickname along the far apron in the trim color; a center-court mark (no marks on the wings: the user pointed out real courts don't have them, Nov 2026). At center court is the home team's official primary logo (decided Nov 2026, the one exception to the no-logos rule in CLAUDE.md): the SVG from NBA.com's CDN, cleaned of scripts and external links and stored as `web/public/logos/{ABBR}.svg`, drawn onto a transparent canvas over the painted center circle, its whites toned to the court's soft line white so they don't bloom. If a logo can't be loaded the site's own crest for the team is drawn instead (`lib/crests.ts`, `components/scene/crest-art.ts`; all 30 on `/dev/crests`): its own shape, layout and simple symbol in the team's colors. Courtside on the far side: a scorer's table with a lit strip in the home color and the benches. The stands rise in tiers along the far side and both ends, every seat the same dark slate, tiers and seats unlit. The fans sit in them, after the audience in the `kyledev223/lions-share` site: each a head and shoulders drawn in fine dust, all one color per side (the away team's glow color on the left half of the arena, the home team's on the right), lit from above, the front rows catching the court's light and the upper rows fading into the dark; about one seat in eight is empty. White camera flashes, rafter lights, a dim blue haze and faint white dust. No screens, no colored lights on the players. Each player stands on the court: an overhead light, high and a little behind them, casts each one's own animated shadow forward from his feet onto the floor (a shadow-receiving layer over center court, since the painted floor is unlit), with a tight dark contact shadow right under the feet. The camera shifts slightly with the pointer (parallax).
- **The cursor and the lens** (decided Nov 2026, after lions-share). Mouse users get a crosshair (`components/cursor.tsx`): a thin ring and a dot exactly on the pointer; over anything clickable the ring grows and turns red, over a player who can be picked it grows further and reads PICK, it tightens while the button is down. In the 3D scene a lens follows the cursor (`components/scene/lens-effect.ts`, a post-processing effect): inside a small circle (120 px across at 1080 px tall) the picture magnifies a little, ripples outward and splits red from blue at its edge, fading in while the cursor moves and out a second after it stops. The fans near the cursor hop as if cheering and brighten (`fanMaterial`, each fan moving as one). On the Predict screen the lens covers the page's own layers too, so everything there swims under the cursor: the nav, the matchup line and team names, the hint, the games drawer and the disclaimer each get an SVG displacement filter that follows the cursor with the same magnification and ripple (`components/home/ui-lens.tsx`; layers marked `data-lens`; attached only while the lens shows; no color split, which would thicken text edges). The results panel is left out, since a filter would turn off its frosted-glass blur. Other pages have no lens. Touch and reduced motion get neither.
- **Glow colors:** each team's rim and floor ring glow in its jersey color, brightened (`hologramColor`); if the two would look alike, `hologramMatchup` switches one team to its alternate (or turns the away glow cyan).
- **Post-processing:** bloom (threshold 0.66, so only light sources glow) and a vignette. Fine film grain over every page (CSS).
- **UI: the hologram site's mix of 3D and regular web.** Huge, wide, heavy uppercase headings; small mono labels (e.g. `TONIGHT · 7:30 PM ET`) in NBA red; thin glowing accent bars; dark glass panels with a hairline edge and faint scan lines (`holo-panel`). Every page sits on the same backdrop: near-black with a blue glow overhead, a red one low, faint stars. Team names over the players are small (the players are the hero).
- **Everything responds; nothing is boxed** (decided Nov 2026). Outside the home page there are no framed panels: stats, charts, lists and tables sit straight on the page, set apart by space and their mono labels (the home page's game cards and results card keep their glass). With a mouse, a soft glow follows the pointer across every page but home (`components/interactions.tsx` writes `--px`/`--py` on `<html>` only, so streamed content is never touched before it hydrates); the home page's glass panels show a spotlight where the pointer is. Report-card numbers count up when they come into view and glide when the season changes (`components/count-up.tsx`), and glow on hover; the season switch is a pair of text tabs with a sliding underline. Page content rises into place as it scrolls in (a CSS scroll-driven animation; browsers without view timelines show it as is). Nav and footer links draw a blue underline; the nav wordmark has the site's ball mark, which spins on hover. Rows in tables and lists light up under the pointer, the game page's reason bars grow in and dim the others on hover, and its last-10 games are a strip of results marked by a green or red top edge. Reduced motion turns all of the motion off; touch gets the reveals only.
- **No name; the mark is a basketball** (decided Nov 2026). The site is titled "NBA Game Predictor" and shows no wordmark: the nav on inner pages has only the mark, a plain solid ball with its seams cut out (`components/ball-mark.tsx`, in the text color). The icons are drawn from the same paths by `web/scripts/make-icons.mjs`: `app/icon.svg` (black, white in dark mode), `favicon.ico` (16/32/48 px with a thin white edge so it reads on any tab), `apple-icon.png` and `public/brand/avatar.png` (black on white; the second is for the Vercel project avatar, which Vercel stored from an old favicon and has to be uploaded by hand under Settings → General). No Next.js badge in development (`devIndicators: false`).

### Typography
- Display and body: **Archivo** (variable). Headings use its width axis at 125% and weight 800+ (wide, heavy, uppercase), like the reference site.
- Labels: **JetBrains Mono**, small, uppercase, tracked (`eyebrow` utility).
- Jersey lettering on the 3D uniforms: **Oswald** (condensed, like real jerseys); not used for page text.
- Numbers: tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) in all stat tables.

### Color tokens
- `--bg: #02040A`, `--panel: rgba(8,14,32,0.62)`, `--line: rgba(243,246,252,0.14)`
- `--text: #F3F6FC`, `--text-muted: rgba(243,246,252,0.66)`
- NBA colors: `--nba-blue: #1D428A`, `--nba-red: #C8102E` (Predict button and primary actions). On black, text and glows use brighter versions: `--blue-hi: #5B8CFF` (links, focus rings, accent bars) and `--red-hi: #FF4D5E` (mono labels).
- `--good: #22C55E` (correct prediction ✓), `--bad: #EF4444` (✗)
- Team colors come from `shared/teams.json` at runtime. Check contrast. If a team's primary color is too dark for text on `--bg`, use its secondary color or white.

## 2. Layout

### Desktop (≥1024px)
```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     Predict   How it works   Performance   Docs   GitHub │  nav (no wordmark on home)
│                           TONIGHT · 7:30 PM ET                            │
│ ▬       BOSTON · 2-0 · AWAY                    NEW YORK · 1-1 · HOME      │  small names
│ ▬           CELTICS          (fans in the stands)     KNICKS              │
│ ━          (hologram)                             (hologram)               │  ticks = today's games
│ ▬            player                                 player                 │
│ ▬        (center circle, both standing on the court)                       │
│                    CLICK A PLAYER TO PICK WHO WINS                         │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
- The canvas fills the viewport; the UI floats over it.
- **Players:** centered, each standing on the court under its team name with a strip of floor below his feet, as big as fits between the (small) names and the one-line hint, with both fully visible (`lib/scene-framing.ts`; tested at desktop, tablet and phone sizes).
- **Picking:** hovering a player pops him out, gently: he grows 3.5% and steps 0.15 m toward the camera with a little spring, his rim brightens and widens (a highlight in the shader, `uHighlight`), a ring in his team color lights on the floor under him with a second ring pulsing outward, and the other player steps back into the shade. The room answers too: the fans on his team's side of the stands light up. The team name's color bar lengthens. Clicking picks. The team names are buttons too.
- **Games: today only.** A slim column of small two-tone ticks on the left edge, one per game in its teams' colors, the selected one longer and lit, with "10 games" set vertically below. With a mouse the drawer slides out when the pointer comes within about 110px of the left edge and tucks away when it leaves; a tap on the ticks opens it on touch; keyboard focus opens it too; Escape closes it.
- **Cards** are flat, in the hologram style: dark glass with faint scan lines, a wash in the two teams' colors, the time (or live/final score) as a mono label, the matchup wide and heavy, and the nicknames with a small glowing dot in each team's color. Hovering (or focusing) pops a card out (slides right and scales up) with a blue glow and a light sweep; the selected card has a red edge. When they don't all fit, the list scrolls.
- **No games today:** "No games today." with a button for the closest later day that has games (e.g. "Show Fri, Nov 21"), which then shows that day's games the same way. If nothing is scheduled, it says so.
- No wordmark and no footer bar on the home page. Other pages keep the nav wordmark and the footer (data credit and GitHub). The "unofficial fan project" disclaimer is not on every screen (the user's decision, Nov 2026): it's said once, on Docs → Limitations (CLAUDE.md, Brand and IP).

### Mobile (<1024px)
- Nav collapses into a menu; same order: matchup line, team names (record on its own line), players (fully visible), the "Tap a player" hint.
- The games drawer opens from the ticks and closes after picking a game or tapping outside it.
- Results: the close-up of the viewed player fills the top of the screen and the stats column scrolls over the lower part, full width, over a shade rising from the bottom; Home is top left and the switch top right under Menu, both above the player's head.

### Idle state
- Both players stand still apart from small movements (decided Nov 2026): one calm idle (`Idle`, breathing and small weight shifts) on a loop with a different start offset each, no idle variants, stretches or other extras, and the head turning a little toward the cursor. The other clips in the model stay unused.
- **Posture** (decided Nov 2026, `components/scene/posture.ts`): the auto-rigged stance hunched the shoulders up and forward and pressed the arms into the jersey, so after the mixer poses him each frame a correction lowers and pulls back the shoulders (11° and 8°), opens the chest (6°, a little more through the spine), lifts the head (5°) and swings the arms 7° off the torso, in the body's own frame. It's applied just before the frame is drawn and taken off right after (`restorePosture`): the mixer saves a bone's current pose when a clip starts or blends, and a correction left on the bones compounded into it. Full strength on idles, 40% during one-shot clips. Tuned in `/dev/jerseys` (sliders, `?close=1` close-up, `?side=1` profile, slow-motion speeds).
- **The jump shot is built in code** (decided Nov 2026, `components/scene/jump-shot.ts`; the Meshy Text to Motion clips held the ball overhead with both arms straight up, and Mixamo has no basketball shot): paths for the hips (a 12 cm dip, a 30 cm jump peaking at the release, the landing absorbed), the torso lean, the head looking up at the rim, the ball (belly, then up the midline to a set point in front of the face) and both hands (shooting hand under and behind the ball, guide hand on its side; after release the shooting arm reaches up and out toward the rim with a wrist flick and holds the follow-through). Two-bone IK turns the paths into arm and leg rotations, with the feet planted until takeoff and pointed in the air. It starts and ends on the idle's first frame and is sampled at 30 fps into an ordinary 2.2 s `JumpShot` clip when the model loads, replacing the one in the file.
- Blending: one-shots fade in over 0.25 s, idles over 0.4 s; the shot hands back to the idle over 0.5 s (seamless, since it ends on the idle's first pose).
- Head look-at: while waiting for a pick, the head and neck turn toward where the cursor points (yaw up to about 25°, pitch about 12°, eased), on top of the animation. Off during the cinematic and with reduced motion.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: animation, hologram effects, dust, the fans' shimmer and the camera flashes hold still (`paused`); cards don't pop or sweep.
- The camera drifts slightly with the pointer (parallax), not on its own.

### Swapping games
- On card click: brief fade of the spotlights (200ms) → swap hologram colors, lettering and spotlight colors → fade up. Players don't reload, so there is nothing to preload.

## 4. Prediction cinematic storyboard

Driven by a single **GSAP timeline** controlled by a **zustand state machine**:
`idle → revealing → shooting → results → returning → idle`.

As built (`components/scene/matchup.tsx`, decided Nov 2026 after the lions-share site's screen changes). The camera moves by blending from the home framing toward the cinematic's shot (`camera-override.ts`); the whole shot's camera is one path driven by a single clock (`placeShotCamera`), so it never stops and starts: an orbit about the shooter's feet in polar form (angle, radius, height), turning the short way with a cosine ease and a gentle pull-back (15%), rise (0.35 m) and widening (3°) at its midpoint, no overshoot; from 65% of the way through the swing a slow drift toward the rim eases in and carries on through the flight; the results shot blends in over 1.4 s while it's still drifting. No letterbox, light cuts or shake (all removed for a smooth move). The shot's props (hoop, ball, trail, sparks) are compiled and their textures uploaded when the scene loads, not when first drawn (that froze the move for 300 ms as the ball appeared). Times are from the click; release and later depend on the outcome. Sped up about 40% (Nov 2026, the user found the camera too slow): the results appear about 4.4 s after the click (was about 6.4), the ball's flight is a little quicker (0.95 s plus a bit for long shots), and players fade in and out faster to keep up with the camera.

### Shot outcome
Let `p` = the model's win probability for the picked team. If the pick is right (`lib/slate.ts` `verdict`):
- `p ≥ 0.70`: clean swish (ball path ends at the rim center).
- `0.58 ≤ p < 0.70`: kisses the back iron, then drops (2-segment path).
- `p < 0.58`: rattles: hits the front rim, bounces up, rolls around, drops in (3 to 4 segments). (This also covers a right pick against the model, once the final score decides.)

If it's wrong (`pickShot` in `lib/shot-path.ts`):
- `p ≥ 0.40`: off the back iron, around the rim and out the side.
- `p < 0.40`: off the front of the rim and back toward the shooter.

### Ball flight math
- Parabola from release point `P0` to rim `P1` with apex height `h = max(P0.y, P1.y) + 2.5m` for a 6.8 m shot, plus 0.2 m per meter beyond (the shot from center court is about 13.9 m); the flight takes 1.1 s plus 0.08 s per meter beyond 6.8 m. Sample it with a quadratic Bezier, and drive `t` with GSAP `power1.in` for slight acceleration.
- Ball spin: backspin around the axis perpendicular to the travel direction, about 2 rotations per second.
- Rattle: precomputed keyframes relative to the rim. Don't use a physics engine.

### Skipping and accessibility
- **Skip** (bottom right; a mono label with a short red underline that runs full width on hover and an `Esc` key hint, no box) and the `Esc` key jump the timeline to the `results` label (the make's sparks still fire, and the camera goes on to the hero shot).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no camera moves. Crossfade to results with a static winner highlight.
- All results content is real DOM (not canvas) so it's readable by screen readers. Announce "Prediction: Knicks win with 64% probability" via an `aria-live` region.

- **Only the viewed team** (decided Nov 2026): every number in the tabs is about the team being viewed. The other team's stats appear only after the corner switch ("See the Knicks →"); nothing in the panel switches teams or shows both teams side by side.
- **Three tabs** under the top (text with a sliding underline in the team's glow color, arrow keys move between them; they keep their place when switching teams). The chart types were chosen in Nov 2026 to replace an earlier set (a ring, fill bars, a radar, mirrored bars and range cards) that read as too samey:
  - **The team** (named after it): season, home, away and last-10 records as small **donuts** (the share won in the team's color, the record in the middle); streak and rest as chips. Scoring as a **dumbbell** on a 95–130 scale (points scored and allowed per game, the line between them green or red by the sign of the margin). The last 10 as a **line chart** of points scored (team color) and allowed (dashed white), oldest first, the gap between the lines shaded green where they won and red where they lost; pointing at a game names the opponent and the score. "Advanced · where they rank": all 13 advanced stats (offensive, defensive and net rating, pace, true shooting, eFG%, turnover rate, offensive rebounding, free-throw rate, and on defense opponents' eFG%, forced turnovers, defensive rebounding and opponents' free-throw rate) as **rank strips**, each a rail with a notch per team in the league (1st on the right, the middle third faintly lit) and the team's dot on it, green in the top third and red in the bottom third (pace isn't colored); pointing at one explains the stat.
  - **The game:** "How the model got there", full width: the reasons as a **waterfall** in the viewed team's terms, from an even game (50%) each reason a step toward the team it favors (in that team's glow color), then "Everything else the model weighs" landing on the prediction; the axis zooms to the steps' range and a dashed line marks 50%. Step sizes are the stored factors' approximate effects (`game_predictions.factors`), so the note says they're approximate. Then the season series (each game's winner bold) and the tip-off time.
  - **Players** (when the viewed team has lines): a **box score**. On top, the team's three projected leaders (scoring, rebounding, playmaking): the number large in the team color, the player's number and short name, the 80% range and once final the real number. Then a table of its projected players, sorted by minutes and re-sorted by clicking a heading (minutes, points, rebounds, assists, TS%, USG%): minutes as the number over a bar out of 48, and points, rebounds and assists each as the projection beside a **whisker** on the column's shared scale (the 80% range as a capped line, the projection as a dot, once final the real number as a tick, green inside the range and red outside), the range in faint mono under it with the real number and ✓/✗. Injury status and "Didn't play" show under the name. A key explains the marks; the note says the lines assume he plays. The table scrolls sideways when narrow; on phones the leaders shrink into one row.
- Charts answer the pointer, focus and a tap by filling a caption line under them; the caption is `aria-live`, and every mark has a text equivalent for screen readers. With reduced motion nothing grows or draws in.
- **Home** sits top left in the nav's row (the nav lets clicks through its empty middle on the home page), above the stats.
- **The switch:** a button in the bottom corner of the player's half, never over the stats ("See the Knicks →", underlined in their color; on phones top right under Menu) views the other team. The camera glides to its player, who appears as if he'd been picked from the start, and every number changes; the eyebrow then reads "If you'd picked the Knicks" and the headline "That one wins." or "Not that one." Focus moves to the new headline and the area scrolls back to the top.
- **Phones and portrait screens** (under 768px wide, or taller than wide, where the camera centers the player): the player in the top part, the stats scrolling over the lower part, full width, one column, a smaller ring.
- The verdict is red when wrong, white with a blue glow when right; the team name is underlined in its color. Finished games show the Final badge as colored text.

## 7. Motion and performance budgets
- Home JS (excluding three.js chunk) under 150KB gzip. The three.js and scene chunk loads after first paint.
- `player.glb` under 4MB (meshopt-compressed). Each jersey texture set under 400KB (WebP or KTX2, 1024²).
- Target 60fps on a mid-range laptop and 30fps or more on recent phones. Use drei's `<PerformanceMonitor>` to drop DPR and post-processing when needed.
- The loading screen (`components/arena-loader.tsx`, used while the slate streams and while the 3D chunk loads, and in How it works chapter 5): the mark in white dribbling on a faint floor line, its shadow growing and shrinking under it, on the 3D room's navy glow (so the scene takes over without a jump), with a mono label ("Loading tonight's games", "Setting up the arena") and a red hairline sweeping under it. It fades in after 0.25 s so quick loads don't flash it; it holds still with reduced motion. `/dev/loader` shows it on its own. Display team names and the pick buttons immediately so the page is usable before 3D is ready.
- **Phones start light** (`components/scene/arena-scene.tsx`, viewport under 768px): density 1 and no post-processing. Bloom and vignette are added only after 8 seconds of smooth frames (50 fps or more, no decline from `PerformanceMonitor`), and the bloom fades in over about half a second. Desktop starts with them. Any device that keeps struggling drops them.
- **Startup work is spread out:** the reflection map (a small room rendered six times and blurred) is built when the browser is idle after the scene appears, not in the first commit; shaders go through `compileAsync` with rendering paused (in parallel where the browser supports `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`); the WebGL check is a feature test, not a throwaway context (a failed real context still reaches the 2D fallback through the error boundary).
- **The film grain is a static noise tile** (`public/textures/grain.png`), not a live SVG noise filter with an overlay blend: blending a fixed layer over the animated canvas every frame was the single biggest cost on the page.
- **Fonts:** Archivo and JetBrains Mono are preloaded; Oswald (jersey lettering only) isn't, and the 3D player suspends until it has loaded so the lettering is never drawn in the fallback face.
