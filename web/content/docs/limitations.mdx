Every model has blind spots. These are this one's.

## It doesn't pick more winners than Elo

On the held-out 2025-26 season the model's win chances beat a simple Elo rating clearly (log loss
0.587 against 0.598, an edge that holds up statistically), but Elo still picked slightly more
winners (69.2% against 68.1%). The model is built to get the chances right, not to win a pick'em.
The [performance page](/performance) keeps score on live games.

## It knows the injury report, not the final word

The model reads the league's official injury report as of 9 AM Eastern, and again at 5:45 PM for
games that haven't started. A player ruled out after that, a late scratch at warm-ups, or a minutes
limit isn't known. In the morning about 1 team in 5 hasn't filed yet (mostly the second night of a
back-to-back), and for those teams only "missed the last game" counts. "Questionable" is a coin flip
the model treats as one: about half of those players play. Reports only go back to 2019-20, so the
model learned what they're worth from five seasons.

## Lineups and minutes are invisible

The model sees teams, not rotations: rest decisions, minutes limits and late scratches only show
up after the fact, through recent form.

## Some games are odd

- Neutral-site games (international games, NBA Cup semifinals) get no home-court edge, and for
  past seasons the designated home team isn't known.
- The 2019-20 bubble and the shortened 2020-21 season are in the training data, with their unusual
  home-court numbers.

## Probabilities, not certainties

A 70% favorite loses about 3 games in 10. Being "wrong" on a 55% pick is expected roughly half the
time. Judge the model on many games, as the performance page does, not on last night.

## Scope

Regular-season games only (no playoffs or play-in). Player projections assume the player plays and
cover each team's 8 players with the most projected minutes.
