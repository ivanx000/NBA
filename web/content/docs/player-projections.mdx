Every game also gets projected lines for each team's 8 players with the most projected minutes:
**minutes, points, rebounds and assists**, each stat with an 80% range. They're published with the
game prediction, before tip-off, and graded once the game ends: each game's page (linked from the
results screen and the [performance page](/performance)) shows the projection next to the real line.

Projections mean **"if he plays"**. The players projected for a game are the ones who played in
their team's previous game, adjusted by the league's official injury report as of 9 AM Eastern on
game day (and again at 5:45 PM, for games that haven't started): players listed **Out** or
**Doubtful** aren't projected, players listed as available after missing the last game (back from an
injury) are, and anyone listed as Questionable or Probable carries that tag next to his name.

## Two stages

Points are minutes times points per minute, and the two are driven by different things, so they're
modelled separately:

1. **Minutes:** recent minutes (last 3, 5, 10 and 25 games, and how much they vary), whether he's
   been starting, rest, games he's missed, teammates who are out (more minutes for the rest), and
   blowout risk (a big rating gap means starters sit in the fourth quarter).
2. **Per-minute rates:** points, rebounds and assists per 36 minutes over recent windows, shot
   volume, usage, and the opponent's defense, pace and rebounding.

Projection = minutes × rate. Each stage is a LightGBM regressor; the rate models weight each game by
its minutes, so a two-minute stint doesn't count as much as a full game.

```text
points = projected minutes × projected points per minute
```

Models that predict each stat directly were tried as well. The two-stage version was slightly
better for all three stats on the validation season, so it's the one used.

## Usage from the box score

Usage (the share of a team's possessions a player finishes while he's on the floor) is computed
from the box score rather than fetched:

```text
usage = (FGA + 0.44 × FTA + TOV) × (team minutes / 5)
        / (minutes × (team FGA + 0.44 × team FTA + team TOV))
```

That keeps the daily cloud job self-sufficient: it only has cdn.nba.com box scores.

## The ranges

Separate quantile models predict the 10th and 90th percentile of each stat. A conformal correction,
learned on a season those models never saw, then nudges the ranges so that about 8 in 10 real lines
land inside. In practice the correction was tiny: the quantile models were already close to 80%.

## The injury report's share

Four inputs come from the report: his own chance of playing, the minutes his teammates are expected
to lose (all together, and the biggest single absence), and the minutes coming back from injury.
A teammate out for weeks counts for nothing new: his minutes are already spread around. On the
2024-25 validation season they cut the minutes error from 4.77 to 4.69 (the last-10 average misses
by 5.11), with points, rebounds and assists a little better too; the minutes gain held in each of
the four seasons checked.

## Against the baseline

The bar was each player's average over his last 10 games. Settings were chosen on 2024-25, then
the models were refit and scored **once** on 2025-26 (23,170 player-games), as the game model was.
These are the second version's numbers, with the injury report (re-tested once as a new major
version); the first version scored 4.73 on minutes and 4.73 on points.

| | Last-10 average | Model | Better by | 80% range held |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes | 5.08 | 4.66 | 8.3% | |
| Points | 4.88 | 4.71 | 3.6% | 80.9% |
| Rebounds | 2.00 | 1.93 | 3.4% | 80.7% |
| Assists | 1.44 | 1.40 | 3.0% | 82.2% |

(Mean absolute error: the average miss, in minutes or in points, rebounds or assists.)

The model beats the baseline on every stat, but not by much. A player's recent average is a strong
baseline and single games are noisy: the typical miss on points is still almost 5. Most of the edge
comes from minutes, from things the last-10 average can't see (rest, who else is out, a likely
blowout).

This season so far (a weekly walk-forward backtest: each week from models trained only on earlier
games), the edge is the same: minutes 6.7% better, points 3.6%, rebounds 2.7%, assists 2.8%, with
the ranges holding 79–80% of real lines.

## Limits

- **Who plays:** a player who missed the previous game isn't projected, even if he's back tonight,
  and a late scratch still gets a line.
- **Opening night and trades:** a player counts for his new team only once he's played for it.
- **No jersey numbers yet:** the game logs don't carry them, so players are shown by initials.
