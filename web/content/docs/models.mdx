## How models were compared

- **Splits by time:** train on 2015-16 to 2023-24 (10,749 games), tune and calibrate on 2024-25,
  and test once on 2025-26. Nothing is ever chosen on test numbers.
- **Rolling validation picks the champion** (since model v3, Nov 2026): each season from 2019-20
  to 2024-25 is predicted by the candidate trained only on the seasons before it, and the lowest
  pooled log loss wins. One season (1,230 games) turned out to be too few to choose between models
  this close.
- **Primary metric:** log loss, because the site shows probabilities, not just picks. Brier score,
  accuracy, AUC and calibration error are reported too, always next to the baselines.
- Every run is logged to MLflow with its parameters, metrics and model.

## Candidates

| Model | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Home team | Always picks home, at the training home win rate (56.8%) |
| Elo | The Elo win probability on its own ([Features](/docs/features)) |
| Logistic regression | All 61 features, standardized; regularization picked on validation (C = 0.1) |
| LightGBM | Tuned with Optuna (100 trials); settles on very shallow trees |
| Ensemble | A log-odds blend of the two; its best weight was 0.8 logistic regression |
| **Elo-anchored** | Starts from Elo's log-odds and learns adjustments from 37 features (rest, travel, availability, lineup strength, the injury report, last-10 form, home and road records), with a penalty on the adjustments only, so noisy inputs can't pull it far from Elo |

## Reading it honestly

On its test, plain Elo edged out the first model (a gap of +0.004, not statistically meaningful).
Rather than swap in Elo because of test numbers, which would turn the test season into a tuning
set, the models were studied on six earlier seasons. The first model's edge over Elo there was
small and lost in three of the six. Two changes fixed it: anchoring on Elo, and a lineup-strength
feature ([Features](/docs/features)). The official injury report then added the clearest gain so far,
on the earlier seasons first and then on the re-test. Elo is still graded next to the model on live
games, on the [performance page](/performance).

## In production

The champion is refit on every finished game since 2015-16 and registered in MLflow as
`tipoff-game@champion`; its calibrator is fit out of time on 2025-26. A weekly job refits it and
promotes the refit only if it's no worse on the latest four weeks than what was published.
