A probability is only useful if it means what it says: of all the games the model gives 70%, the
favorite should win about 70%. That's calibration, and it matters more here than raw accuracy,
because the site shows the number.

## How it's done

- The raw logistic regression is passed through **Platt scaling** (a one-variable logistic fit on
  its log-odds).
- Calibrators are fit **out of time**: on one season's predictions from a model trained only on
  earlier seasons, the way the model is used live.
- Platt beat isotonic regression for every candidate model; isotonic was the worst every time. The
  fitted map is close to identity (slope about 1.01), so the logistic regression was already nearly
  calibrated.

## On the test season

![Reliability diagram for 2025-26: predicted probability on the x axis, observed home win rate on the y axis; the model's points follow the diagonal closely.](/docs/figures/calibration_test.webp)

Expected calibration error on 2025-26 was 0.033 for the champion and 0.045 for Elo (lower is
better): the model's probabilities were slightly better calibrated, even though Elo's picks were
right a little more often.
