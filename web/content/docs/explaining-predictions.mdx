Every prediction on the site comes with its reasons: "Celtics better rested", "Knicks in better
recent form", with how much each one moved the probability. These aren't guesses made after the
fact; they're an exact split of the model's own calculation.

## An exact split

The champion is a logistic regression, which adds up its inputs in log-odds:

```text
logit(p) = base + Σ coefficient × standardized feature
```

So each feature's contribution is just its term in that sum, and the contributions plus the base
equal the prediction exactly. (For LightGBM the same split comes from TreeSHAP, the algorithm in
`shap.TreeExplainer`; Platt scaling multiplies every term by its slope.) Tests check that the parts
add up to the whole.

## From 56 features to 11 reasons

The 56 contributions are summed into **11 factors** defined in `shared/factors.json`, each with
plain-language copy:

| Factor | Copy (for the team it favors) |
| --- | --- |
| Team rating | Celtics rated higher overall (Elo) |
| Home court | Home court and home/road records favor Celtics |
| Season-long strength | Celtics stronger over the season |
| Recent form | Celtics in better recent form |
| Shooting | Celtics have the shooting edge |
| Turnovers and rebounding | Celtics win the turnover and rebounding battle |
| Pace | The expected tempo suits Celtics |
| Rest and schedule | Celtics better rested |
| Travel | Knicks on a tougher trip |
| Missing players | Knicks missing key players |
| Point in the season | The point in the season favors Celtics |

(Travel and missing players name the opponent: the Knicks' tougher trip favors the Celtics.)

Each game stores its top 5 factors (those worth at least half a point).

## From log-odds to percentage points

Log-odds aren't intuitive, so each factor is shown in **win-probability points**:

```text
pp = contribution × (p − p_base) / (logit p − logit p_base)
```

where `p_base` is the model's prediction for an average game. This keeps every sign, and the
factors add up to `p − p_base`. It's an approximation (probability isn't linear in log-odds), but a
faithful one.

## A known quirk

The Elo difference includes the home-court edge, so part of home advantage shows up as "Team
rating" rather than "Home court". Correlated groups (Elo, season strength, recent form) also share
credit in ways that can look arbitrary. Splitting Elo into a pure rating gap and a home edge would
make the reasons cleaner; it's on the list.
